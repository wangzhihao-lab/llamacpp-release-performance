# Step 1b: change-point detection (E-Divisive means)

Generated by `scripts/analysis/step1b_changepoints.py`. Reads `results/<tag>/`, `candidate_events.csv`, `config_changes.csv` and `data/releases.csv` only; it builds nothing and runs no benchmark.

## Method and parameters

- **Statistic**: for each (tag, series), the median of the 5 `samples_ts` of the campaign run (avg_ts when samples are missing), natural log, ordered by build number. Only rows with `param_set == main` and `status == ok` are used. qwen fails to load at b1778 and earlier and at b2761, so those points are absent.
- **Robust noise** σ = 1.4826 · MAD(first differences) / √2 (log units, one value per series, from all points).
- **Isolated spike**: a point that differs from the median of its 2 neighbors on each side (only 1 on the short side next to an end; the two end points are not judged) by > 4σ, while the medians of the left and right neighbors differ by < 2σ. Spikes are listed in `spikes.csv` and left out of the change-point detection (a single pass, no iteration).
- **E-Divisive** (Matteson & James 2014, JASA 109:334-345), α = 1, minimum segment length 3, R = 999 permutations, accepted when p < 0.01, κ search mode `full`. How the implementation maps to the formulas of the original paper:
  - For a cluster Z_1..Z_T and 1 ≤ τ < κ ≤ T, X_τ = {Z_1..Z_τ} (n = τ) and Y_τ(κ) = {Z_τ+1..Z_κ} (m = κ − τ);
    Ê(X, Y; α) = 2/(mn)·Σ_i Σ_j |X_i − Y_j|^α − C(n,2)⁻¹·Σ_{i<k} |X_i − X_k|^α − C(m,2)⁻¹·Σ_{j<k} |Y_j − Y_k|^α; Q̂ = mn/(m+n)·Ê; (τ̂, κ̂) = argmax Q̂ with n, m ≥ 3. In the code, `scan_splits` takes the three double sums by blocks from a two-dimensional prefix sum of the distance matrix (`cross`, `within_x`, `within_y`) and computes all (τ, κ) in one vectorized step. Ties go to the smallest τ, then the smallest κ. The self-test checks this against a direct double loop.
  - Hierarchical bisection (`e_divisive`): with k−1 accepted change points, the best split of every cluster is computed and the cluster with the largest Q̂ proposes the k-th change point τ̂ (κ̂ only bounds the right sample and does not become a change point).
  - Significance (`permutation_test`): the observations are permuted independently within every cluster and the maximal Q̂ over all clusters is recomputed, R times in all; p = (1 + #{Q̂^(r) ≥ Q̂}) / (R + 1). With p < 0.01 the point is accepted and the procedure continues, otherwise it stops. The generator is `numpy.random.default_rng(zlib.crc32(series name))`, so results are reproducible. All change points in this report use the full R permutations. In the synthetic self-test and the κ variant, a split stops early once its rejection is certain ((1 + exceedances)/(R + 1) ≥ 0.01). This changes no accepted change point (an accepted split never reaches the stopping count, and a rejection ends the procedure) and only makes the p of the final rejected split a lower bound.
  - Relation to existing implementations: the double (τ, κ) search, U-statistic normalization and within-cluster permutation are the same as in e.divisive of the R package ecp. MongoDB's signal_processing_algorithms (PyPI 2.1.6, checked by reading its source) fixes κ at the end of the window, normalizes within-cluster sums by n² (V statistic), uses p = #/(R+1), accepts when p ≤ the threshold and has no minimum segment length. Hunter (Fleming et al. 2023), according to its abstract, replaces the permutation test with a t-test to remove the randomness of the results. This script keeps the permutation test (fixed seed) and also runs the variant with κ fixed at the end of the cluster as a comparison (see the robustness section).
- **Change-point output**: the level of each adjacent segment is the median of the log statistic in it; Δ = exp(right − left) − 1; |Δ| < 5% is marked small (kept, but not put on the re-measurement list); |Δ|/σ uses the log difference.
- **Gradual change**: the point next to the boundary (c−1 | c) on each side is a transition point if it lies strictly between the two levels and farther than 2σ from the level of its own segment, and the transition interval is then widened past it by one point on that side. span_old and span_new are the tags where the transition starts and ends (without a gradual change, the last tag of the left segment and the first of the right). The interval is widened by one step only. If a widened end meets the same condition it is flagged `transition may be wider` (csv column transition_may_extend). Multi-step widening is not used because slow drift inside a long segment biases its median, and multi-step widening would run through undetected plateaus or dips (in a trial run the transition of qwen/cpu/tg128 extended into the two-point dip at b4464).
- **Merging across series**: change points whose transition intervals (b_span_old, b_span_new] overlap (sharing only an end point does not count) are merged into change intervals R01, R02, ... (the same merging rule as step 1).
- **Comparison with step 1** (per change point): (i) the change interval overlaps a step-1 event and the same (model, back end) is listed in step 1 with a tag pair that overlaps this transition interval (step 2 runs these binaries, and the other test is measured along with it); (ii) the change interval overlaps a step-1 event but this group is not listed with an overlapping tag pair; (iii) no step-1 event overlaps the change interval.
- **Regression not recovered**: a falling change point with |Δ| ≥ 5% that no rising change point of similar size follows in the same series (similar size: ln rise / |ln fall| within [0.5, 2]).

### In reverse: where the step-1 events (54) fall

Series-level match: the tag pair of a series in a step-1 event overlaps the transition interval of a change point of the same series → on a change point. ## Limitations

- Only changes between sampled releases can be seen. A transition interval lies between sampled tags and does not locate a commit.
- σ is estimated from the MAD of adjacent differences and is too large when a series has many steps (especially tinyllama/metal), which makes the spike and gradual-change criteria conservative.
- The permutation test assumes that observations within a cluster are exchangeable; slow drift or autocorrelation across releases makes p too small. The hierarchical bisection tests step by step without a correction for multiple comparisons.
- Each point is a single campaign run, so segment levels and Δ come from the medians of single runs. Whether a change is real depends on the interleaved re-measurement of step 2.
